# Practitioner interview and pilot draft

Prepared 4 October 2026. Draft for founder use; no invitations have been sent. Use a synthetic demonstration and avoid asking for restricted records.

## Candidate interview

Suggested length: 25 minutes. Talk to people who gather, evaluate or review threat reporting, and to someone who owns the software budget. Do not assume the practitioner and procurement owner are the same person.

Opening: “We are prototyping an evidence review workspace. It organizes source excerpts, contradictions and cited assessments. It does not forecast attacks. We would like to understand how this work is done today using a non-sensitive example.”

Questions:

1. Describe a recent non-sensitive reporting task from first source to approved brief. Which steps and tools were used?
2. Where was the most time spent: finding material, checking attribution, comparing reports, integrating data, writing, or coordinating review?
3. How do you distinguish reposts from independent corroboration and handle corrections or conflicting reports?
4. What metadata must survive a handoff? What makes a brief unacceptable to its reviewer?
5. Who assigns follow-up work, approves a judgment and owns overdue tasks? Where do handoffs fail?
6. Which exports or public sources can be used in a test, and who can authorize reuse and retention?
7. What hosting, access, offline operation and integration constraints would block adoption?
8. For AI assistance, which bounded task is useful, what evidence must accompany the output, and which errors would make it unusable?
9. Who would sponsor a pilot, judge its results and approve a purchase? What steps does that decision require?

Record observed examples separately from assumptions: current task, frequency, approximate effort, error/rework examples, existing alternative, required integrations, permission owner, pilot sponsor and objections. Do not interpret polite interest as willingness to purchase.

## Proposed pilot

One analyst question, one approved dataset and one reviewed brief. Start with synthetic or permitted non-sensitive sources, including a repost, a contradictory account and a correction. No live alerts or operational decisions.

Agree baseline and acceptance criteria before the trial. Compare the same work with the existing process and with Ruthless: elapsed analyst effort, missing/incorrect source references, preserved contradictions, number of reviewer corrections and successful recovery of the workspace. Record sample size and limitations; no quantitative benefit claim is justified without measurements.

Stop or narrow the test if source rights, data authority or recovery cannot be established. Production acceptance is a separate process. A useful demo is not a government procurement award.

Founder inputs needed for an actual pilot: a named contact and sponsor, explicit permission to reach out, an approved sample, the reviewer and an agreed scope. These are later business decisions; they do not block current synthetic development.
